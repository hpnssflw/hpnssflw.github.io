SMTP delivery for the research agent was fully built — and never sent a single message.

## What to watch for

- "Built" isn't "working": subject/body assembly and the send call
  shipped, but `SMTP_HOST`/`SMTP_USER`/`SMTP_PASSWORD` were never added
  to `agent/.env`, so every run hit a graceful no-op instead of an inbox.
- Stale docs keep dead decisions alive: "add SMTP credentials, send a
  live email" stayed written down as the next step long after the
  decision to drop it, so it kept resurfacing as if it were still live
  (author's notes).
- A visible counter isn't action: the dashboard showed the pending
  count from early on, but it still climbed past a hundred over a
  month — only a new channel cleared it, not the visibility.

## Why it's interesting

- The channel got picked before there was a reader to deliver to —
  twice: once abandoned for an on-page dashboard, once for Telegram —
  and neither pivot touched the queue underneath.
- Decoupling collection/ranking from delivery made the channel
  swappable: replacing SMTP with Telegram meant rewriting two files in
  place, not restructuring the pipeline.

## In practice

[`agent/pending.py`](https://github.com/hpnssflw/hpnssflw.github.io/blob/main/agent/pending.py)
is the part that survived both pivots unchanged. Delivery itself moved
from SMTP to Telegram in
[`agent/deliver.py`](https://github.com/hpnssflw/hpnssflw.github.io/blob/main/agent/deliver.py) —
same queue, different channel.
